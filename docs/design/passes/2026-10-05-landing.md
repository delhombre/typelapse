## Decisions

Foundations (`emil-design-foundations`):

- Static frames lay their lines out as a flex column (`.studio-code code`),
  which drops the whitespace text, and each `.line` keeps `min-height: 1lh`.
  Leading is now 15.2px for 9.5px code, 16.8px for 10.5px (measured).
- Hero meta: each fact is a `whitespace-nowrap` span and the separator is held
  to the fact before it (`hero.tsx`, `META`), so a wrap never splits a fact
  or opens a line on a dot.
- `.lp-h2` 700 to 600, the display's weight. Size, tracking and balance kept.
- One plain sentence per section line: diff ("Paste a unified diff or a
  GitHub pull request, and Typelapse turns the change into editable scenes on
  the timeline."), social, export ("One clip, five cuts: ..."). The morph line
  kept: its second sentence is the WYSIWYG claim. No claim, number or price
  changed; "seven themes" still stands in the spec sheet.
- CTA heading "Paste a change and make your first clip.", so the button is
  the only place that says "Open the studio".
- Spec sheet values in sentence case with a full stop.
- `.lp-island-caption` takes `text-wrap: balance`.
- Kept: no headline in the hero (decision 2026-08-09), the numbered steps,
  the 80 / 96 / 112 rhythm, the 17px lead, the blue focus ring (D9).

Material (`emil-marketing-pages`): the page already passes its checklist,
nothing changed for it. Intro once per session (`sessionStorage`), no
scroll-triggered entrance (non-immediate `Reveal` is a plain `div`), Geist
and Ioskeley preloaded, nothing advances without the visitor, CTA right for
signed-in and signed-out alike (no account needed).

Orchestrator's calls after the review, applied the same day:

  one box the size of every ratio at once (`frameBox`: 636 × 366 at full
  scale) and the ratio is a `clip-path: inset(... round 18px)` on it
  (`frameClip`), driven by the same `gentle` spring (stiffness 120, damping
  18) through `useTransform`. A clip cuts its element's own shadow, so each
  ratio keeps a still shadow layer of its own size underneath; the outgoing
  one fades out in 120ms, the incoming one fades in over 350ms after 200ms,

  shadow's empty middle as a pale card round the frame mid-spring (seen on
  the shot, fixed). A resize jumps instead of springing.
  Trace of one 16:9 to 1:1 change with the morph paused

  13.4ms, from the click to 1.43s** before; **7 events, 1.2ms** after. The 7
  are the click itself (the label text, the helper tooltip mounting), the
  start and end of the shadow fade, and three 0.1ms passes on the island's
  aspect glyph; none per frame of the spring.

**The fan on a phone.** Three faults on the shot, not one:

- The row was not edge to edge. `FrameFan` kept its own `lp-gutter` inside
  `ExportSection`'s container, so the row's `-mx-6` took back one gutter of two:
  it ran from x=24 to x=351 at 375 (327px), cut its cards' shadows in a visible
  band at both ends, and the second card began 2px past the cut, so nothing said
  the row scrolls. The focus rule under it already said "runs edge to edge".
- The first card floated: a 328px row around a 169px card, 76px of paper above
  and below.
- The shadows reach 60px under the cards and the row had 8px.

Decided by looking, with live tweaks on the built page: the bleed alone; the
baseline (`self-end`); the 9:16 first with centre, baseline and top alignment;
the cards in descending height with baseline and centre. Top alignment hung a
short card from the row's ceiling, centre left every short card floating in its
own slack (the 16:9 last in the row in 75px above and below), and the baseline
is the fan's own (`bottom-4`) and keeps the slack above the short cards. The
order came from the first screen: it has to show the card that sets the row's
height, and two different shapes. Descending height (9:16, 3:4, 4:5, 1:1,
16:9) was the cleaner staircase and was tried first, but a 3:4 second ends at
435px (24 + 180 + 16 + 215), inside a Pro Max's 430 to 440, and the row read as
complete with nothing to swipe to. With the 16:9 second the second card runs
off the edge at every width (360, 375, 390, 430, 440 checked).

What changed: `ROW_CARDS` (the tallest first, then the fan's own order),
`self-end`, no second gutter, `.lp-fan-row` takes the whole gutter back with
`max(var(--lp-gutter), env(safe-area-inset-*))` on each side and keeps 1rem
above and 4rem under its cards as padding given back as a negative margin (the
shadow room), `overscroll-x-contain` (a swipe at the row's end does not chain
into history navigation), `select-none` on the fan's and the row's cards (code
built illustrations, `emil-ui-polish` 12). The desktop fan did not move: its
five card rects are identical at 1440 before and after.

Measured after, 375 x 812: the row is 375 x 400 at x=0 (was 327 x 328 at x=24),
the first card at x=24 and 320 tall, all five bottoms within 1px of 336. No
horizontal overflow at 320, 360, 375, 390, 430, 640, 768 and 1023; the row is

The morph holds the display's rate at 4x for every ratio and on the phone setup
(1:1 and the phone's 16:9 and 1:1 included, all 0% over 20ms). It first misses
at 16x, at the same point for both ratios (16:9 at 98.2fps with 7.1% over
20ms, 9:16 at 98.8fps with 5.1%), and at 24x both run at 69.7fps. The
renderer's main thread is 17 to 19% busy at 4x (16:9 and 9:16) and 31 to 32%
at 8x. The clip does cull: the raster work for 9:16 is 58% of 16:9's on
33% of the area on the desktop, 42% on the phone setup, so the worry that the
frame paints its mesh at the full 636 x 366 under every clip was wrong. The
main thread's style, layout and paint cost is the same at every ratio (the
background string is written once a frame whatever the clip). No frame drops, so no fix: nothing changed in the
instrument. Not verifiable here: the throttle slows the main thread only and
the raster ran on this Mac's cores, so a phone's GPU raster is not covered.

**The theme after an error.** The cause is where the chrome lives: the page's
hook set `data-landing-theme` and `theme-color` and its cleanup took them down
as the failure unmounted it, while the fallback is still the landing's route.
`useLandingChrome` is now the two effects on their own, and the route's error
fallback mounts `LandingChrome` when the page it replaces was the landing (it
reads the attribute at the moment of the throw, as it already did for `.dark`,
and the attribute wins over a `.dark` a studio visit left on the root). The
fallback's effect runs after the page's cleanups (React runs every cleanup of
a commit before any new effect), so the document keeps the page's theme and
lets go of it when the route is left. A landing that never mounted gets the
theme-color too. A failure after mount, in a real browser: the theme toggle
writes storage inside a state updater, so a refused write throws during render
and the boundary catches it (`143/chrome-after-error.mjs`, a dark landing on a
light OS, 375 x 812):
