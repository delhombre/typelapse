# UX benchmark: what Polar, Dub and the code-video tools teach Typelapse

Date: 2026-10-12. Method: code and copy of `polarsource/polar` and `dubinc/dub` (shallow clones, read, deleted), public pages of dub.co/pricing, polar.sh/docs, snipcast.dev, codeimage.dev and ray.so fetched as text. No app was run, no account was created. Typelapse facts come from the files named in each row.

## Verdict

Typelapse already has the hard thing the benchmarks lack: a studio that works at once, with no account and no upload. What it does not have is a way to tell a stranger what to do next, what an account is for, or what the paid side is. Polar and Dub spend their effort exactly there: an opening fork that names the two paths, a short checklist that always shows the next step, an upgrade prompt that fires on the control that was pressed (not on a settings page), usage shown as a meter with a reset date, and a return from checkout that says what changed. Typelapse says almost none of this today. The empty studio gives one sentence ("Capture the code to add your first scene."), the Cloud option under Where is invisible until the visitor has signed in (`useCloudRenderAvailable` in `cloud-render.tsx`), so a free visitor never learns hosted renders exist, and the return from checkout is a toast, "Creator is on.". The fixes are mostly small copy and placement changes, not new systems. Caution on transfer: Polar and Dub are B2B dashboards that gate everything behind sign-up; Typelapse's advantage is the opposite (no wall), so we borrow their guidance and their upgrade moments, never their sign-up wall. Snipcast.dev is the counter-example: "Get Started Free" jumps to pricing and both plans are "Start 2 Weeks Trial", so the product is not tried before the account.

## What the benchmarks do, by area (evidence)

Onboarding
- Polar `app/(main)/onboarding/start/page.tsx`: "Welcome to Polar", "What are you looking to do?", two cards: "I'm just exploring" ("Jump into a sandbox environment with test data and mock payments. No real money, no setup required.", button "Launch sandbox") against the real setup. The fork is stated, the safe path is the first card.
- Polar `OnboardingChecklistCard.tsx`: a progress bar, "{completed} of {total} required steps complete", and only the next step with a title and one line (`stepLabels.ts`: "Create your first product" / "Something for customers to buy"; "Set up checkout" / "A checkout link or API integration so you can get paid"). It ends on "You're ready".
- Dub `ui/layout/toolbar/onboarding/onboarding-button.tsx`: a "Complete setup" toolbar button; each task is title plus description ("Create a short link" / "Create your first Dub short link", "Explore analytics" / "View clicks and performance data"). The onboarding flow itself (`app/app.dub.co/(onboarding)/onboarding/(steps)`) has a `later-button.tsx`, so every step can be skipped.
- Dub `ui/links/links-container.tsx` empty state: title "No links yet", description "Start creating short links for your marketing campaigns, referral programs, and more.", the create button inline, and a "learn more" link to a help article.

Account value and sign-in
- Dub `ui/auth/register/signup-oauth.tsx`: "Continue with Google" and "Continue with GitHub" before the email form; register page "Create your Dub account", "Already have an account? Log in".
- Polar names the account's value through the sandbox: you can use the product with test data before committing.

Upgrade and paywall
- Dub `ui/shared/upgrade-required-toast.tsx`: fires where the limit was hit. Title "You've discovered a {Plan} feature!", one explanatory line, one full-width button "Upgrade to {Plan}" (default link `/{slug}/upgrade`).
- Dub `ui/layout/sidebar/sidebar-usage.tsx`: usage bars always visible in the sidebar, a warning at 90% of any limit, "Usage will reset {billingEnd}", and a primary "Upgrade plan" button shown only on the free plan or when near a limit.
- Dub `ui/layout/upgrade-banner.tsx`: a slim banner for exceeded limit, failed payment ("Your last payment failed. Please update your payment method") and canceled subscription.
- Dub pricing page (dub.co/pricing): headline "Flexible plans that grow with you", Monthly/Yearly switch with the yearly saving stated, one tagline per plan (`packages/utils/src/constants/pricing/pricing-plan-taglines.ts`: "For small teams or creators needing advanced link features"), a "Best Value" badge, a grouped comparison table, "Start 14-day trial", and a FAQ of eight questions (exceeding limits, cancelling, refunds, privacy).

Checkout and after
- Polar `packages/i18n/src/locales/en.ts`, `checkout.confirmation`: processing state "We are processing your order" / "Please wait while we confirm your payment.", success "Thank you for your order!" / "You now have access to {product}.", failure "A problem occurred while processing your order" / "Please try again or contact support." The webhook delay is a designed state, not a silent gap. `CheckoutBenefits.tsx` then grants and shows what the buyer got.
- Dub `ui/modals/upgraded-modal.tsx`: opens on `?upgraded=true`, waits for the workspace to refresh, then "Dub {plan} looks good on you!", "Thank you for upgrading to the {plan} plan. You now have ... higher usage limits", a list of what unlocked, and "View dashboard". It deletes `upgraded`, `plan`, `period` from the URL on close.
- Polar `DashboardSidebar.tsx` and `CONFIG.IS_SANDBOX`: a top banner on the sandbox and a "Go to Sandbox" / production switch in the account menu. Test mode is always visible.

Understanding
- Dub `pricing-plan-main-features.ts`: feature tooltips carry `{ title, cta, href }`, so a tooltip can end in a link. `ui/layout/sidebar/news.tsx`: dismissible "what's new" cards (six shown, dismissed ones remembered). `help-button.tsx`: a persistent Help button in the sidebar.
- Polar docs home: a four-step path (create account and organization, first product, choose an integration shown as tabs, webhooks), and a "Set up Polar with your coding agent" modal (`OnboardingChecklistCard/AiSetupModal.tsx`) that copies a ready prompt.

Navigation and settings
- Polar `components/Dashboard/navigation.tsx`: sidebar groups (Products, Customers, Analytics, Sales, Developer, Finance, Settings), with Billing a child of Settings. Dub: `settings/billing/plan-usage.tsx` holds plan, usage, "Manage plan", pending cancellation ("Resume subscription"), invoices and payment methods on one page.

Code-video peers (landing pages only, nothing was operated)
- snipcast.dev: nav has Templates, hero offers "View Examples", plans are $12 monthly and $99 yearly ("Most Popular"), no free tier, trial needs an account. Templates are the show-and-tell.
- codeimage.dev: hero button "Getting started" opens the app, "Login" is a header link, "20+ custom themes", open source. Static code images, so the lesson is only: the first click lands in the editor.
- ray.so: headline "Create beautiful images of your code", nothing else visible on the fetched page; not used as evidence.

## Typelapse today (from the code)

- Empty studio: a sample snippet titled "Untitled", no scenes. Real strings: "Capture the code to add your first scene." (`step-builder.tsx`, line 207), "Capture a scene to animate this code." (inspector hint), and for Render "A video needs at least one scene." / "Write some code, then capture it as a scene." (`render-block.ts`). The brief's "Edit the code, then Capture" is not a string in the code.
- The six starters live behind a button labelled "Showcase" in the island (`template-gallery.tsx`: "Six real Typelapse projects. Explore the motion, then make one yours."). Import a pull request is under "Add source" > "Import a change" (`studio-source-hub.tsx`).
- Sign-in panel: eyebrow "Typelapse Cloud", "Continue your work.", "Sync projects and private media, then render in the background.", button "Email me a code", then "Check your email." (`studio-account-control.tsx`). The signed-out local panel says "Private by default. No account required." and links to "About Typelapse".
- Plan card: "Current plan", "Private cloud + render compute", cadence buttons with prices from `/api/billing/catalog`, "Upgrade to Creator" (`AccountBilling`). `upgrade-button.tsx` is still unmounted and its title text says "Pro", "watermark", "4K/60fps", which are not our rules.
- Allowance: one server-built sentence, "You are using 1 of 3 cloud projects and ... ; hosted renders come with Creator." (`allowanceSentence`, `plan-allowance.ts`).
- Export tab: "Where" offers This device, Server (only when offered) and Cloud (only hosted and signed in). The cloud status row has the only other "Upgrade to Creator" (`cloud-render.tsx`).
- After checkout: `/app?billing=success` gives the toast "Creator is on." after polling the plan up to 20 s, or "Payment received. Your plan updates in a moment."; `cancelled` gives "Checkout cancelled, nothing was charged." (`billing-return.ts`).
- Help: `StudioTooltip` and `title=` attributes, an "About Typelapse" link, keyboard shortcuts hook with no visible list. No changelog, no help entry.

## The three to ship first

1. Row 1, the empty-state next step. It is the cheapest change (S) on the screen every visitor sees, and it fixes the one measurable failure in the first minute: a dead sentence where a path should be. It also makes the existing gallery and PR import discoverable without building anything new. Row 2 follows once row 1 proves the pattern.
2. Row 5, make the paid side visible where the free export stops. Revenue depends on a free visitor learning that captions and audio exist on hosted renders at the moment they notice the device render lacks them. Today that moment gives a caveat and no way forward. Dub's pressed-control prompt is the model, and it does not touch the free device path or add a watermark, so it respects the product rule.
3. Row 8, the confirmed return from checkout. The staging proof (#71) ends exactly here, and a paying customer's first impression of Creator is this screen. It is S because the polling, the state machine and the URL cleanup already exist in `billing-return.ts`; only the surface and the copy change.

Rows 3, 6 and 9 are quick (S) and can ride along with these. Row 7 waits on the final prices (#79) for its numbers but its structure can be built now from the catalog.

## Limits of this research

Polar and Dub are operated through dashboards; none of their screens were run, so animations, timing and empty states that exist only at runtime are not covered. Dub's and Polar's public pages were read as fetched text, and the competitor pages (snipcast.dev, codeimage.dev, ray.so) are landing pages only, so nothing here says how their editors behave. snipcast.io was fetched by mistake (a podcast product) and is not used.
