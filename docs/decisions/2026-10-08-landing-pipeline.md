# The landing becomes a pipeline, drawn in hairline figures

Bruno, 2026-10-08, after the design audit: "redesign the landing to improve
it; if possible, in addition to images, use isometric figures like
hairline.lucasmarkes.com". Three directions were prototyped
(https://claude.ai/artifact/RP3Fwsuu2pa51S5n4utwe5): Pipeline, Manual,
Studio. Bruno: "Vas pour pipeline".

## D1 The page is the pipeline

**Decision:** the landing reads as one line from a pull request to a file:
the hero (headline, the import field, the live engine beside it), a strip of
the five steps, then five stages on a vertical rail (Paste, Morph, Check,
Export, Hosted), each pairing an isometric figure with real product imagery;
then the measured sharpness proof, pricing in two columns, a final call to
action. The specification table from the Manual direction joins the proof.
`frame-fan` and `capability-ledger` leave.

**Why:** the reader is a developer between two tasks who sees the page
once; the product is in the first screen and the story runs in the order
of use. Manual was twice as long; Studio leaned on a centred hero.

## D2 Our figures, on hairline's engine

**Decision:** five Typelapse figures (scenes, glide, safezone, local,
queue) drawn with the `hairline-create` skill on hairline's kernel, which
is copied into the repo with its MIT notice. They read the warm ramp, keep
one bright stroke, never the yellow, answer the pointer and the finger,
and land at rest under reduced motion.

**Why:** hairline's 33 figures are generic objects; ours draw what a
screenshot cannot show. MIT is compatible with AGPL-3.0 when the notice
travels with the code.

## D3 The hero stays the engine

**Decision:** the prototype's looping video was a stand-in; the live hero
keeps running the real engine (WYSIWYG, D12).
